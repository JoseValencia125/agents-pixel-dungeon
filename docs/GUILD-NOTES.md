# Guild implementation notes

One entry per milestone: what changed, what was decided, and how it was checked. Newest last.

## M0 — Hardened core (2026-10-14)

- **State and events commit together.** `Store` opens nested units of work (`BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, then savepoints), and `Service::atomic` wraps every mutation that writes state and appends events: task, attempt and quest transitions, dispatch, candidate commit, candidate selection, integration, permission answers, creation of quests, tasks and members. Events appended inside a unit are held and sent to subscribers only after the outer commit; a failed unit rolls back both and sends nothing. Test: `a_failed_unit_of_work_leaves_neither_state_nor_event` (also nested units).
- **The user's checkout is never written.** The game design is no longer exported into the working tree: `design.export` commits `game-design/` onto the managed branch `refs/heads/guild/design/<project>` from a temporary detached checkout (`workspaces::publish`, compare-and-swap on the ref) and `project.add` reads the design back from that branch (`workspaces::read_ref`) before looking at the checkout. The user merges the branch when they want it.
- **Open harness ids.** `HarnessKind` became `HarnessId`, a validated slug; the registry maps ids to adapters and the core never names a harness. Adding one is a new adapter file and a `register` call.
- **Failure and recovery invariants.** `Attempt.generation` increases on every (re)drive and a driver's result is ignored when the attempt moved on (`attempt.stale_result`). Reconcile finishes an integration the service died in (idempotent: the squash finds nothing new), puts a submitted candidate's task up for a decision, and settles a race whose runners were lost. Tests cover dying while provisioning, waiting for a permission, verifying and integrating; a race losing one, two or all runners; a stale result; and three restarts in a row.
- Found on the way: two reentrant store locks (a `MutexGuard` kept alive by an `if let` scrutinee) in the stale-result and shutdown paths.

## M1 + M2 — Execution strategies, an independent judge, durable decisions (2026-10-14)

- **`ExecutionStrategy` on every task**: `{type: single | reviewed | candidate_race | experiment, candidates, selection: user | policy | judge, auto_integrate}`. `candidates` and `auto_integrate` on the task are derived from it; the older flags still map (`--candidates N` is a policy-selected race). `reviewed` is one attempt a judge looks at.
- **Hard gates before any choice.** Only candidates whose required verifiers passed (`passes_gates`) can be selected, by anyone. Then the selection: the user on the board, the policy (smallest passing change, then the earliest), or a judge.
- **The judge is a role on any harness.** A judge attempt (`AttemptKind::Judge`) goes to a free member with the `judge` role (then `reviewer`), preferably not an author of the candidates. Its prompt carries the task, the acceptance criteria, the design summary and each passing candidate's diff under an anonymous label (A, B, C in an order derived from a hash of the attempt id), never the authors. It must answer a fenced JSON block with scores on the rubric (correctness, acceptance coverage, simplicity, maintainability, architecture, unnecessary complexity, design alignment), findings, trade-offs, winner, confidence and reason; the winner is a judgement, not a sum.
- **`RaceDecision` is durable** for every selection, by the judge, the policy or the user (with `human_override` when the user chose against an earlier decision), with the anonymous candidates, scores, findings, winner, confidence and reason; also written as a `race.decision` artifact of the judge attempt. Events: `judge.started`, `judge.finished`, `race.decided`. CLI: `task new --strategy race --candidates 3 --select judge`, `decisions <task>`.
- **Fallbacks keep the guild moving**: an unusable or lost judge (no JSON, unknown label, failure, restart) falls back to the policy and the decision says why; a task never gets two decisions.
- Found on the way: integration picked "the latest passed attempt", which after a judge was the judge itself; selection and integration now consider work attempts only.

## M3 — A row per attempt (2026-10-14)

- **`AttemptRecord`**, written in the same unit of work as `attempt.finished`: task, role, strategy, candidates, selection, attempt kind, member, harness, model, result, attempt number, duration, turns, resumes, files and lines changed, verifier counts, judge score, winner or loser, who decided, human override, failure. A later decision updates the rows of attempts that had already finished. Usage (tokens, cost) has a slot and is empty until an adapter reports it.
- Read with `records.list {projectId?, taskId?}` or `guild records`. No learning yet: the point is honest rows for the routing policy to come.
- Real check: two Kiro workers raced on a small Python change, a Codex judge that had written neither scored them 32 and 35 on the rubric, named B with confidence 1.0, and B was integrated alone; `guild decisions t1` shows it.

## M4–M8 — Hypotheses, experiments, playtests, telemetry, analyst (2026-10-14)

- **`Hypothesis`** (problem, evidence, level whole/loop/part, hypothesis, expected effect, proposed change, measurements, status proposed → experimenting → supported/rejected/inconclusive) and **`Experiment`** (variants with parameters, a playtest plan, a result, the human's decision). `experiment.start` makes a quest with one single-attempt, policy-selected, never-auto-integrated task per variant plus an analysis task that depends on them; the variant builders get `GUILD_PARAMS: {…}` to apply exactly. No second scheduler: the builds are ordinary tasks and candidates.
- **Playtests** run the project's `[playtest] argv` (`{profile}`, `{seed}`, `{runs}`) in each variant's worktree, for every profile and seed of the plan; the raw `game.event` lines are kept whole as an artifact and derived into counts, means of numeric fields, run completion and the choice distribution (`top_share` is what a dominant strategy looks like). A restart resumes the plan where it was.
- **Telemetry contract**: `{"type":"game.event","runId","event","data"}` per line on stdout; common names `run.started`, `round.started`, `choice.presented`, `choice.selected`, `damage.dealt`, `enemy.defeated`, `run.completed`, `run.failed`; projects add their own.
- **The design analyst** is an attempt of kind `analysis` on a member with that role (then judge, reviewer, implementer), fed the hypothesis, the design, the variants and the aggregated metrics; it answers a JSON verdict (supports / rejects / inconclusive, evidence, unexpected, recommended variant and next step, confidence) that becomes the `ExperimentResult`, moves the hypothesis and leaves the decision to the human: `experiment.accept` integrates one variant's build, `reject`, or `iterate` (a new draft).
- **AI players** in this slice are the fixture's own deterministic profiles (greedy, novice, optimizer, explorer) in `fixtures/wordfight/`; an LLM-driven player that observes and acts through a game API is a later phase.
- Test: `an_experiment_builds_variants_playtests_them_and_the_analyst_answers_the_hypothesis` runs the whole loop on the word game with the mock harness (3 variants × 2 profiles × 2 seeds).

## M9 — The living game design (2026-10-14)

- Source: Sellers' *Game Concept Document Template* and *Detailed Game Design Documentation Template*. The `LivingGameDesign` carries the concept template's sections (working title, concept statement, genre, audience, USPs, player experience and POV, visual and audio style, world fiction, monetization, platform and scope, core loops, objectives and progression, systems, interactivity) and the detailed template's progression: current status, key moments, systems with intent and present-tense design, deeper sections (world, UI, art, objects, technical, tools, localization, ideas) only when a project has something to say, unresolved and resolved questions kept apart, and a maturity per section. Whole → loops → parts stays inside it.
- **Current design and design history never mix.** The design holds what is true now; `DesignQuestion` (Q-NNN) and `DesignDecision` (DEC-NNN) are their own entities, with hypotheses, experiments and playtests as the evidence between them. A question is never deleted: resolved, it points at its decision; the decision points at its evidence and the systems point at both. Accepting an experiment writes a decision (playtests as evidence, other variants as rejected alternatives) and resolves the question its hypothesis came from.
- **No invented detail.** The prompt context tells agents that what is marked unknown is not decided and must come back as a question; the chamber's systems have an **Ask** per unknown.
- **The repository copy is linked Markdown** (`overview.md` as the tip of the iceberg, then one page per section, loop, system, object, question, decision, hypothesis, experiment) plus `design.json`, the structured state the guild reads back; still published to the managed design branch, never the checkout.
- Design chamber in the dungeon: eight tabs (guild, concept, experience, loops, systems, parts, questions, decisions), a maturity bar and counters. Test: `a_question_is_resolved_by_a_decision_the_design_points_at_and_both_survive_a_restart` covers the first milestone's eight points.

## M10 — Play the build, observe, and the laboratory (2026-10-14)

- **Play sessions** (`play.start` / `play.finish`): a human `Playtest` with its build commit, design version, optional experiment variant, player, a detached checkout under the guild's worktrees (or the variant's own worktree) and a telemetry file the game appends to through `GUILD_TELEMETRY`. The dungeon runs the project's `[play] argv` in a terminal window (`Terminals::run`, through `/usr/bin/env` so the variable is there from the start) and closes the session when the program exits; the CLI runs it in place. Finishing keeps the raw events as an artifact, derives metrics and removes the checkout; it is idempotent.
- **Observations** (OBS-NNN): kind (confusing, frustrating, interesting, great moment, bug, comment), text, and the links read from the session and its telemetry at that moment: build, design version, experiment, variant, run, round, event index. Promoting one creates a question raised by "playtest" whose source is the observation; it is never deleted. Accepting an experiment counts its human playtests and lists its observations in the decision's evidence; the analyst's metrics include a `human` profile per variant.
- **Signals** are deterministic, from raw events only: dominant choice (≥50% of ≥8 choices), an attribute of the presented options that the chosen one nearly always maximises (≥70% and ≥25 points over chance; string options count their length), options offered ≥5 times and never chosen, completion ≤34% or 100% of ≥5 runs. Each suggests a question; the human opens it or not. Nothing is concluded for them, and no LLM is involved.
- **Laboratory** in the design chamber: hypothesis and experiment forms, progress while building and playing, and the decision card (problem, evidence, analyst verdict, per-variant numbers with **▶ Play**, accept / iterate / reject with a note).
- The word-combat fixture gained a `profile=human` mode for the terminal and a `[play]` command.
- Checked: unit test of the signals, a service test of the whole play loop on the fixture (session, a mid-session observation tied to run and round, signals finding "the longest word wins", promotion to a question linked to its system), a pty test that the game gets its telemetry variable and its end is reported once, a headless render test of the Laboratory and Play tabs on the demo, and three real sessions played through the CLI.
